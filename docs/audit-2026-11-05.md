# Implementation audit, 2026-11-05

Scope: the whole repository at commit `f955de7` (`Offer ES256 before EdDSA for new passkeys (#85)`). Read-only; no code was changed. `npm run typecheck` is clean and `npm test` passes 57 files, 613 tests (two unattributed workerd `AbortError` lines appear in the output as noise; see L13).

Method: the entry Worker, IP keying, upload tokens, WebAuthn, config and Web Push modules were read in full by the lead. The Durable Object's auth/authz paths, the SQLite store, the cost-control machinery, the streaming/push/presence features, and the CI/scripts/docs surface were each reviewed in depth by a dedicated reviewer against SPEC.md, and every high or medium finding below was re-verified at the cited lines. Findings marked **(verified)** were confirmed by the lead reading the code; the rest are the reviewers' with their citations.

Overall: the security core is sound. No SQL injection, no cross-principal sign-in, no SSRF, no secret leakage to clients or logs, correct WebAuthn handling, correct Web Push cryptography, correct private-room gating on every posting, reaction, history, membership and listing path but one. The real problems are in the places the protocol leaves to the deployment: the edge-stop trip is far cheaper for an attacker than the outage it causes, a few admin operations run outside the serialization the rest of the code relies on, and several recovery paths fail open or get stuck.

## High

### H1. One address can turn on the global edge stop for 30 minutes with one minute of cheap traffic (verified)

**Status (2026-11-05):** Option A applied: the flood trip's hold is 5 minutes and the attack pause's first hold 10 minutes (still doubling to 4 hours), so a sustained flood re-trips while a burst costs everyone minutes, not half an hour. Option D stays an operator decision.

`src/index.ts:1576-1583` calls `watchForFlood` on every request, before the per-address, per-/48 and per-location limiters. `src/budget-guard.ts:216-232` samples one request in `floodSampleEvery` (20) into the per-location `FLOOD_WATCH` limiter (600/min) and trips `apron_budget_stop` when it is exceeded; `src/plans/paid.ts:217-223` sets `floodRequestsPerColoMinute: 12_000` and `holdSeconds: 1800`. Refused requests count. A `GET /ws?apron_connection_status=1` passes the WAF rule (docs/edge-admission.md).

Scenario: one client at ~200 requests/s for one minute (about $0.006 of billable requests) blocks every user of `server.apron.chat` for at least 30 minutes; one minute every 30 minutes keeps it down indefinitely, since the hold does not escalate. This contradicts the stated policy (commit 196154b, "stop only on money"): the per-address limiter already bounds the attacker's cost to the operator, so the stop converts a ~$5/day cost attack into a total outage.

Suggested fix: count only requests that passed `CONNECTION_ATTEMPTS` (so a single address can contribute at most its 60/min), or require the sampled requests to come from many distinct address keys, or make the flood trip a per-address block rather than the zone stop. Keep the bill-cap stop as the money backstop.

### H2. The attack pause is trippable by one address for about $0.03 of traffic

**Status (2026-11-05):** Option A applied: the flood trip's hold is 5 minutes and the attack pause's first hold 10 minutes (still doubling to 4 hours), so a sustained flood re-trips while a burst costs everyone minutes, not half an hour. Option D stays an operator decision.

`src/burn.ts:45,62-68` prices an entry refusal at 5.448e-7 USD; the fast rule (`burn.ts:138`) trips at $0.0333 in two minutes, about 61,000 refused requests. `src/index.ts:1557-1569` samples refusals ×100 and reports them; `pauseForAttack` (`index.ts:5979-6002`) then closes all sockets and refuses everything for 30 minutes, doubling to 4 hours on repeats (`burn.ts:147-149`). An attacker needs ~510 requests/s for two minutes from one address. With H1 and this together, about 250k requests a day (~$0.12 of operator cost while inclusions last) buy ~7.5 hours of outage.

Suggested fix: as H1, weigh refusals per distinct address key, or exclude refusals from one address beyond its own limiter window from the global burn.

### Proposal for H1 and H2

Both share one root: the flood trip and the attack pause key on volume per location or per server, not per source, so one address with a one- or two-minute burst buys a 30-minute stop (up to 4 hours for the pause) for everyone. The per-address limiter already bounds what one address can make the object do; the only cost a burst imposes past it is Worker invocations, about $0.30 per million, so these stops exist to bound money, and the defect is the ratio of outage to the money at stake.

Options, least change first:

- **A. Shorter first holds, re-trip on persistence (recommended).** Flood trip `holdSeconds` from 30 to 5 minutes; attack pause `firstHoldMs` from 30 to 10 minutes, still doubling per UTC day to 4 hours. A sustained flood re-trips within a minute of the lift, so the outage lasts while the money does; a burst costs everyone 5 minutes instead of 30. Each re-trip lets about a minute of flood through, roughly 12,000 requests or $0.004. Two constants in `src/plans/paid.ts` and `src/burn.ts`, the `holdSeconds >= 15 * 60` assertion in `test/budget-guard.test.ts`, and the figures in docs/configuration.md and docs/limits.md. Low risk.
- **B. Require the flood to persist before tripping.** Trip only when the sampled limiter is exceeded in two different minutes out of three, remembered per isolate (about six lines in `watchForFlood`). Doubles a burst's cost to the attacker; cheap on its own, worthwhile with A.
- **C. Weigh refusals per distinct address in the burn meter.** Not recommended: it would tolerate a single address burning $24 an hour until the bill cap ($5 a day) stops everything for the rest of the day, a worse outcome than a short pause.
- **D. Enable the generated per-address WAF rate rule `apron_connection_attempts`.** Operator configuration, no code: blocks a single address at the edge before anything is billed, so the trip and the pause only ever see distributed floods. docs/edge-admission.md says why it is off by default: the free WAF rate rule cannot filter by hostname, so it counts across every hostname in the zone, and it takes the one free slot.

Recommendation: A now, B with it, and D as an operator decision if single-address floods are seen in practice. None of this touches the bill cap, which stays the money backstop.

## Medium

### M1. A private room's existence can be probed with an empty message (verified)

**Status (2026-11-05):** Fixed: `visibleRoomRow` at the empty-message check, covered by the private-room probe test.

`src/store.ts:7540-7546`: `commitMutation` short-circuits a new message with empty text and no embeds and checks the room with `this.roomRow(roomId)` instead of `visibleRoomRow(roomId, userId)` (`store.ts:7409-7413`), which every other posting path uses (`store.ts:5380`). `handleMessage` (`index.ts:4021-4034`) does no visibility check of its own.

Scenario: any authenticated user, a guest included where guests post, sends `{"method":"message","params":{"room_id":"private_<n>","body":{"text":""}}}` and gets `{}` if the private room exists and `invalid_params "unknown room"` if not. Private ids are `private_<logId>` (`store.ts:7301`), so they are enumerable around public log ids. The probe charges no post, only an 8-read reservation. SPEC §5.3 requires that a private room reads as absent to non-members.

Fix: use `visibleRoomRow(roomId, input.userId)` at `store.ts:7544`.

### M2. `/purge` can leave a concurrently signing-in connection open as a deleted identity (verified)

**Status (2026-11-05):** Fixed: `/purge` closes the user's connections again after the delete and token revocation, and `becomeRegistered` refuses an identity that no longer exists.

`src/index.ts:7164-7169` closes `connectionsOf(id)` first, then awaits `runMutation(purgeUsers)` (7171-7173) and `forgetTokens` (7177). Nothing re-closes afterwards. The sign-in paths (`handleTokenResumeLocked` 3433-3471, `signInKeyless`/`becomeRegistered` 3588-3636) read the identity, await KV writes, then mark the socket registered without re-checking.

Scenario: a mutation is in flight so the purge's `runMutation` is queued; user X's token or session sign-in has passed its identity read and is awaiting KV. The purge's close loop finds no X connection; X's sign-in completes; `purgeUsers` deletes X. X stays connected until it disconnects; its posts carry `from` X (`store.ts:4909`) while charged as anonymous (missing identity row), attributing content to a retired id. `/invite-purge` (7322-7343) does this correctly: it closes in a `finally` after holding the session lock.

Fix: close `connectionsOf(userIds)` again after `forgetTokens`, and have `becomeRegistered` refuse when `store.getIdentity(userId)` is null.

### M3. The idle-account prune does not remove the account's records, so a reissued id inherits authorship

**Status (2026-11-05):** Deferred: `message_state` has no index on `author_id`, so the cheap check at retire time would scan it; sharing the purge's record deletion with the prune would put a log scan into the maintenance budget. The window needs a mod to move a pruned user's message within the retire window.

SPEC §5 says idle accounts are removed "the same way" as `/purge`. `pruneIdleIdentities` (`store.ts:3879-3990`) deletes identity, credential, membership, limiter and dedup rows and writes `purged:<id>` (3966), but no `records`, `message_state` or `reaction_state` rows (compare `purgeUsers` 3781-3811).

Scenario: user `x` is pruned; a mod moves one of `x`'s retained messages (5464-5476 re-logs it, refreshing `latest_log_id`); `dueRetiredUserIds` (4014-4028) frees `x` after `retiredUserIdMs`; a new registrant takes `x`; `message_state.author_id = x` so `commitMessage` (5381-5383) treats them as the author and they can edit or delete the previous holder's message, and reaction rows keyed `(message_id, x)` become theirs.

Fix: have the prune call the same record deletion as `purgeUsers`, or not free a retired id while any `message_state`/`reaction_state` row still names it.

### M4. A failed live-text expiry after a wake is never retried, and dead entries can lock the feature (verified)

**Status (2026-11-05):** Fixed: `liveRecovered` is set only after `expireLive` succeeds, in the alarm and in `recoverLiveTexts`.

`src/index.ts:2590-2591` sets `this.liveRecovered = true` before `await this.expireLive(now)` and swallows its failure (a maintenance-budget `retry_after`). On that path `deadlines.live` is never set, so `recoverLiveTexts` (6648) and `alarmWorkDue` (2651-2660) both skip the probe from then on. `startLiveText` (`store.ts:5722-5725`) counts all message-flow entries toward `2 * concurrent`, so with enough dead entries every `/embed-terminal`, `/embed-append` and live `message` answers `retry_after` "Live text is busy" with a 1 s retry, until a later full alarm happens to run cleanup.

Fix: set `liveRecovered` only after `expireLive` succeeds, or in the catch set `deadlines.live.soonest(now + retry)`.

### M5. Account analytics that return empty groups read as zero usage

**Status (2026-11-05):** Fixed: a month with no analytics groups is read as unavailable (the cron's own invocations make an empty month impossible), covered in the account-usage tests.

`src/account-usage.ts:95-103`: `sum()` throws on a missing field but returns 0 for an empty array. Empty `monthWorkers`/`monthPeriodic` give `exceeded = []`, so `setEdgeStop(false)` (`budget-guard.ts:176-178`) lifts a bill-cap stop once past `holdMs`. On the DO side `evaluateTier` (`index.ts:5891-5919`) posts an admin alert per flip and `onStop` (2245-2250) closes all sockets on each newly stopping reading. Intermittent empty results produce one alert and one socket-close storm per minute; persistent emptiness lifts the month cap and billing resumes.

Fix: treat a day or month with zero rows in every dataset as "unavailable", not zero, when the previous reading was non-zero.

### M6. A flood-tripped stop plus an analytics outage is an indefinite outage

**Status (2026-11-05):** Fixed: the flood trip writes a hold mark into the rule like the attack pause, so the guard lifts it once passed even while analytics fail; a trip while the rule is on does not rewrite it.

`budget-guard.ts:161-175`: the `usage_failed` branch lifts only a rule whose description carries a passed attack-hold mark; the flood trip writes no mark (229); `unconfigured` (154-157, e.g. a revoked analytics token) returns before touching the rule. With H1 this means one minute of traffic plus a token rotation leaves the server off until an operator intervenes.

### M7. README and SPEC promise "inside included usage"; the paid plan is sized well past it by design

**Status (2026-11-05):** Fixed in wording: the README, SPEC section 1 and SPEC section 7 now say the Paid budgets run past the share and the bill cap is the bound.

`README.md:52-55` and `SPEC.md:13`, §7 allocations table (800k writes/day, 10k posts) versus `src/plans/paid.ts:132-139` (10.5M/9M/1.5M daily rows) and `:73` (60k posts). At every ceiling the day costs about $10.85, which `paid.ts:29` states honestly ("about $11 a day"); only `billCap` (`paid.ts:197`: $5/day, $25/month) bounds the bill. `docs/configuration.md:151-160` is correct. The README and SPEC §7 are stale and overstate the guarantee.

### M8. An unpinned third-party action receives a secret

**Status (2026-11-05):** Open: pinning needs the action repository's commit id, which this session cannot read.

`.github/workflows/deploy.yml:69` (`apron-chat/apron-pr-bot/deployed@main`) and `.github/workflows/announce.yml:30` (`apron-chat/apron-pr-bot@main`) run with `secrets.APRON_BOT_TOKEN`; announce runs under `pull_request_target`. Anyone who can push to that action repo's `main` can exfiltrate the token and act as the bot in the public demo. Every first-party action is SHA-pinned, so this is the only unpinned code. The Cloudflare deploy token is not exposed to it. Fix: pin to a SHA.

## Informational

- Attachments can legitimately exceed `ATTACHMENT_SAFETY_BYTES` (12,000) for a user in ~150 rooms who sends a read cursor for each within a minute while typing (worst case ~19 KB from the bounds in `connectionAttachment`, `index.ts:910-962`); the connection closes with 1011 and the newest cursor is lost. Not reachable remotely. No secret is stored in attachments; `credential` is a SHA-256 key.
- Session resume's origin binding (`index.ts:3421-3429`) is against the client-supplied `Origin`; inherent to bearer tokens and spec-conformant.
- A live text's `url` is a bearer capability readable from any origin by anyone holding the 128-bit id, including for private rooms; matches §4.5, worth stating in the threat docs.
- `stale_connections_closed` (`index.ts:8154`) logs up to 8 raw `Origin` header values, arbitrary client strings under `ALLOWED_ORIGINS="*"`.
- `.node-version` is 24 while `engines.node` is `>=22`; this run used Node 22.
- Overruns (`noteOverrun`, `store.ts:2611-2653`) charge the excess and trip a breaker but let the operation finish; §7 calls a cap breach a release blocker. Documented design choice.
- The `billedRequestWeight` assumption that analytics `type === "hibernation"` (`account-usage.ts:107-110`) could not be verified offline; if wrong, DO requests are over-counted 20× (conservative).

## Verified correct

- **Entry Worker**: trusted IP from `CF-Connecting-IP` with Pseudo-IPv4 and `CF-Worker` handling (`src/ip.ts`), /56 and /48 keying, forwarding headers stripped before the internal header is set, fail-closed on missing bindings or address, origin check, no body on upgrade, cheap 404/405 elsewhere, `/.well-known/webauthn` from config only.
- **Upload and live write tokens**: HMAC-SHA256 over key/max/expiry, canonical base64url check, constant-time verify, single use via `claimUpload`/`claimLiveText`, type from bytes only, fixed cache headers, `nosniff` and CSP on Worker-served media.
- **WebAuthn** (`src/auth.ts`): identity resolved from the stored credential, challenge bound to connection, action, RP ID and origin, discoverable credentials and user verification required, bounded credential sizes, counter rollback left to the verifier, candidate ids retried against taken ids, adds-a-passkey path keeps the connection's identity.
- **Tokens and sessions**: admin token compared by digest; every token handler denies on an already-registered connection; bot, device, invite and join tokens resolve identity from storage only; sessions origin-bound, handle-checked, deleted on mismatch; revocation closes connections by credential key for devices, bots and `/invite-purge`; invite uses, token rotation and registration caps serialized or transactional; session lock taken before the mutation lock everywhere.
- **Roles and rooms**: admin commands gated by audience; moves, edits, top-level room creation, `setRole` invariants and private-room membership all enforced in the store inside transactions; `visibleRoomRow`/`visibleMessage`/`historySources` gate every path but M1; non-members get the same answer as for a nonexistent room.
- **SQL**: every statement binds user values; dynamic fragments are constants or `?` lists; no LIKE from input.
- **Retention and dedup**: floor and job persisted before deletion, edited messages survive purge of their creation record, dedup keyed `(user_id, request_id)` and written inside the mutation transaction, replay strips stale grants.
- **Schema**: each upgrade one transaction with the version bump inside; failure throws on every wake rather than resetting.
- **Cost control**: tokens only to `api.cloudflare.com`; GraphQL embeds operator values only; HTTP errors, GraphQL errors and malformed data fail closed; edge toggle idempotent and convergent; prices match the list; UTC rollover correct; attack pause persisted; `budget:check` fails CI on drift; dev and prod ratelimit namespaces distinct.
- **Outbound**: the push endpoint is the only user-influenced fetch; https only, no userinfo, port, IP literal, single label, internal suffix or trailing dot; wildcard hosts match by `endsWith(".x")`; `redirect: "manual"` with a 10 s timeout; og URLs are stored, never fetched.
- **Push**: RFC 8291 encryption with a fresh key and salt per push, text cut to 200 code points, VAPID `aud`/`exp`/ES256 correct, subscription keys validated before crypto, per-message and per-user bounds, private membership re-checked at claim.
- **Live text**: single-use write, claim and session in one mutation, UTF-8-safe backspace and limit cuts, non-blocking fan-out with a per-reader backlog cap, stalled writers aborted, `text/plain` with `nosniff` and CSP, config-only redirect target.
- **Alarm**: one scheduler, earliest wins, cleanup cannot overwrite an auth deadline, reschedules in `finally`.
- **CI**: fork PRs cannot deploy or see secrets; deploy needs tests; `persist-credentials: false`; `pull_request_target` in announce runs on merged PRs only and never checks out PR code.
- **Secrets and logs**: no secret in any `console.*` call or client-bound object; logs carry hashed address keys, user ids and counts only.
- **Docs**: all 103 numeric rows in docs/configuration.md and 25+ spot-checked limits in docs/limits.md match the code; all 36 documented variables exist.
